# OvertimeRL review, round 1: what's worth bringing into Grindlog

Date: 2026-11-26. Source: branch `feat/overtime-rl` (folder `overtime-rl/`, commits `0dd040d`..`2bc9fa3`) and
its author's blueprint (`grindlog-vs-overtimerl-blueprint.md`, kept local, not in the repo). OvertimeRL is
a separate app written by a friend that reads Grindlog's `tracker.db` and `raw.db` directly. This file only
judges which ideas are worth adopting; nothing from it has been merged.

## The core problem: positions that aren't there

The Stats API has no car positions, for anyone. Only the ball's spot at each `BallHit`, the pad at each
`BoostPickup` and the car state of your own team are located. OvertimeRL fills the gap by treating the ball's
spot at a player's last touch (or boost pickup) as that player's position, for up to 12–22 s afterwards.
Read-only counts on the real `raw.db` (384 matches with `BallHit`s):

| OvertimeRL's assumption | Real data |
|---|---|
| A player's last touch tells where they are now | Gap between a player's consecutive hits: median 3.9 s, p75 13.1 s, p90 25.8 s |
| `BoostPickup` fills in positions and powers boost steals, pad discipline and kickoff reads | `BoostPickup` in 2 of 384 matches (174 rows) |
| One hitter per `BallHit` (it reads `Players[0]` only) | 5,458 `BallHit`s have 2–4 hitters, mostly 50/50s; the second car is dropped |

Every rotation rule depends on that position guess: third man in net, rushed from net, back post vs near
post, shadow defense, reckless dive, spectator, boost abandonment, hospital pass, kickoff cheat-up. They
produce confident text ("held 3rd-man net coverage") from a ball spot that can be 20 s old, and each moves
the match rating by up to about 0.85.

Other problems that run through the whole thing:
- **Wrong time base**: moment times are `(ts - started_at)`, which counts goal replays, countdowns and
  pauses. Grindlog's goal clock rules (`goal_clock`, `end_clock`) already solve this.
- **Invented values when data is missing**:
  - goal times become `idx * 30 s`;
  - a goal with no spot gets `y = ±3000`;
  - a hit with no location is placed at `(0, 0, 0)`;
  - "clearances" is just `touches_defence * 0.4`.

  Grindlog's rule is to flag what's missing, never to invent it.
- **Players keyed by display name**, not by Grindlog's player keys and `Shortcut` resolution.
- **Coupling**: it reads Grindlog's internal tables directly, and those aren't a contract, so any migration
  can break it. The blueprint's "copy the pure modules almost verbatim" would bring a 1,563-line analyzer
  carrying all of the above.
- **Privacy**: the public branch hard-codes real gamertags and Epic IDs (`squad.ts` alone has 52), against
  `CONTRIBUTING.md`'s privacy rules. Removing them in a new commit leaves them in history.
- **Assets**: `public/cars/*.png` look like the game's own car renders, a licensing risk in a public repo.

## Verdict per feature

### Adopt (real data, small inference)
1. **Saved-shot locations on the goal map.** The recorder already finds each `Shot` statfeed's last hit by
   the shooter for hardest shot, so saving that spot is cheap. Mark dots by real outcome, not xG.
2. **Double commits as a raw count, with no blame.** OvertimeRL's rule (teammates' consecutive hits within
   400 ms and 650 uu; aerial 750 ms and 900 uu) gives about 1.9 per match on the real data. Before showing it:
   - also count a single `BallHit` with two hitters on the same team (OvertimeRL drops these);
   - check a handful of hits against replays or footage.

   Drop the carrier vs intruder split, since who was rotating in can't be seen.
3. **Assists between friends** on the pair page, from `match_goals` assister → scorer. This is the only
   sound part of OvertimeRL's chemistry score.
4. **Epic vs routine saves** from the game's own `Save` / `EpicSave` statfeeds.

### Maybe, after a prototype on real matches
- **50/50 win rate**: a `BallHit` with hitters from both teams is a real 50/50, and "whose team touches
  next" is a fair win rule.
- **Counter-attack goal tag**: the scoring team touched the ball in its own half shortly before the goal.
  This uses only ball spots at hits.
- **Match stepping with `[` / `]`** on the match page.
- Kickoffs: use the backlog's version (who reaches the ball first, goals soon after a kickoff), not
  OvertimeRL's cheat-up and desertion rules.

### Reject
- **0–10 match rating**:
  - a 6.0 baseline plus dozens of hand-picked weights, many of them on the guessed positions;
  - compressed toward 10 with a sine curve;
  - never checked against anything.
- **xG**:
  - its coefficients are invented, not fitted (shot speed dominates: +0.015 logit per km/h);
  - it can't see defenders or the keeper.

  Revisit only with replay data and a fit to real outcomes.
- **Momentum wave**: Grindlog's Tilt covers it with better ball data.
- **8-axis radar, OVR, EA FC cards and tiers, archetypes, PlayStyles, the 10 awards, the coach report**:
  more hand-picked weights on top of the above. The coach report presents the guesses as advice. A fun share
  card should be built from real stats.
- **5-pillar chemistry score and link tiers**: keep only the assists-between-friends part (adopt item 3).
  Shrinking a small sample toward a prior is a sound idea, but here it wraps unsound pillars.
- **Session podium by rating**: falls with the rating.
- **Boost features** (steals, pad ratio, small-pad discipline): no data (2 of 384 matches). The backlog's
  boost pickup item already says to check `raw.db` first.

## The bar for later rounds

Replay analysis (next round) is where the positional rules could become valid, since replays hold every
car's position; see the backlog's replay analysis item. Each feature brought in should come with:
1. the exact fields it reads, and what it does when one is missing (flag it, never invent a value);
2. 3–5 real moments where the rule fired, checked against the replay or footage;
3. the game clock and Grindlog's player keys, not wall-clock time and display names;
4. no real names, IDs or local paths committed.

For goal clip capture, the open questions are storage size and the recording source, not the analysis.
